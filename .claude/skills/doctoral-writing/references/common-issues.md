# Common Issues in Doctoral Writing

A catalog of recurring challenges in academic writing with concrete diagnostic examples and actionable solutions. Use this catalog to identify systemic error patterns and elevate manuscript prose.

> **Role**: Systematic issue typology and remediation catalog (WHAT). For governing principles, see `SKILL.md`; for practical transformation patterns, see `academic-quick-reference.md`; for extended dissertation case studies, see `before-after-examples.md`; for verification, see `clarity-checklist.md`.

---

## 1. Wordiness and Redundancy

### Redundant Expressions

Eliminate modifiers that add no distinct semantic value to the head noun:

- "past history" → "history"
- "end result" → "result"
- "future plans" → "plans"
- "advance planning" / "advance warning" → "planning" / "warning"
- "first and foremost" → "first" or "foremost"
- "completely eliminate" → "eliminate"
- "close proximity" → "proximity"
- "each and every" → "each" or "every"
- "mutual cooperation with each other" → "cooperation"
- "basic fundamentals" → "fundamentals"
- "consensus of opinion" → "consensus"
- "period of time" → "period" or "time"

### Wordy Phrases

Replace bloated multi-word idioms with direct lexical equivalents:

| Wordy Phrase | Concise Replacement |
|--------------|---------------------|
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | currently, now |
| in the event that | if |
| for the purpose of | for, to |
| with regard to | regarding, about |
| in spite of the fact that | although, though |
| it is important to note that | [state directly or eliminate] |
| has the ability to | can |
| is in a position to | can |
| taking into consideration | considering |
| in a timely manner | promptly |
| a large number of | many |

### Before/After Examples

- ❌ "In this research, analyzing the data collected through the survey administered to the research participants revealed that the following facts came to light."
- ✅ "Survey data analysis revealed three principal behavioral patterns."
- **Conciseness**: 68% reduction in word count with sharpened empirical focus.

- ❌ "Due to the fact that the sample size was limited, it is important to note that the results should be interpreted with caution in light of the fact that generalization may not be possible."
- ✅ "The limited sample size requires cautious interpretation because generalization may not be possible."
- **Words reduced**: 39 → 15 (62% reduction).

## 2. Weak Verbs and Nominalization

### Weak Verb Constructions

Replace generic linking verbs and stock combinations with rigorous domain verbs:

| Weak Phrase | Strong Analytical Verb |
|-------------|------------------------|
| is able to | can |
| make a decision | decide |
| conduct an investigation | investigate |
| perform an analysis | analyze |
| give consideration to | consider |
| have an effect on | affect, influence |
| carry out an evaluation | evaluate |
| draw a comparison between | compare |
| provide an explanation for | explain |
| reach a conclusion that | conclude that |

### Excessive Nominalization

Converting active verbs into abstract nouns buries the core action and inflates sentence length:

- ❌ "made the discovery that" → ✅ "discovered that"
- ❌ "reached the conclusion that" → ✅ "concluded that"
- ❌ "provided a description of" → ✅ "described"
- ❌ "conducted an assessment of" → ✅ "assessed"
- ❌ "offered an examination of" → ✅ "examined"

## 3. Passive Voice Overuse

### Unnecessary Passive Constructions

Avoid unmotivated passive voice that obscures agency or creates bureaucratic stiffness:

- ❌ "Data were collected through surveys. The surveys were administered to 200 participants by the researchers, and statistical analysis was performed."
- ✅ "We surveyed 200 participants and performed statistical analysis on the resulting data."

### Legitimate Uses of Passive Voice

Passive voice is fully justified under three specific conditions:

1. **Unknown or Immaterial Agent**: When the agent is unknown or irrelevant to the analytical point ("The parchment was manufactured in the fifteenth century.").
2. **Focus on the Recipient or Action**: When the recipient or outcome warrants primary emphasis over the actor ("Participants were randomly assigned to treatment and control conditions.").
3. **Disciplinary Conventions**: Standardized laboratory protocols and chemical synthesis reporting in STEM fields ("Spectra were acquired at 298 K with a 500 MHz spectrometer.").

## 4. Unclear Subject-Verb Relationships

### Excessive Separation Between Subject and Verb

Keep the grammatical subject and its main predicate proximate to preserve processing fluency:

- ❌ "The study, which was conducted over a three-year period involving multiple data collection phases at five different research sites across urban and rural areas, examined the relationship between..."
- ✅ "This three-year study examined the relationship between... The research involved multiple data collection phases at five sites across urban and rural areas."

### Vague or Dummy Subjects

Eliminate expletive openings ("It is...", "There are...") that hide the real conceptual actor:

- ❌ "It is clear that there are many factors that contribute to this phenomenon."
- ✅ "Multiple factors contribute to this phenomenon."

- ❌ "There was a significant difference found between the two test cohorts."
- ✅ "The two test cohorts diverged significantly."

## 5. Excessive Sentence Length

### Multi-Clause Run-On Sentences

Long sentences loaded with subordinate clauses degrade comprehension. Decompose compound assertions into sequential units:

- ❌ "Prior studies have shown that social support plays an essential role in mental health outcomes, especially among vulnerable populations, and some researchers have suggested that the type of support may be just as important as the quantity of support received, which provides important implications for intervention design, but very few studies have investigated the specific mechanisms through which different types of support operate, particularly in chronic illness contexts where support needs fluctuate over time."
- ✅ "Prior studies show that social support substantially shapes mental health outcomes among vulnerable populations. Emerging evidence suggests that support type matters as much as volume, bearing direct implications for intervention design. However, few investigations have examined the mechanisms governing different support modalities, particularly within chronic illness contexts characterized by fluctuating needs."

## 6. Poor Transitions and Flow

### Abrupt Thematic Shifts

Ensure consecutive sentences possess clear thematic progression:

- ❌ "The data demonstrate a significant correlation (r = .45, p < .01). The sample consisted of 150 undergraduate students."
- ✅ "The data demonstrate a significant correlation (r = .45, p < .01). This correlation was observed across a sample of 150 undergraduate students."

### Missing Paragraph-to-Paragraph Bridges

Bridge overarching concepts between adjacent paragraphs:

- ❌ [End of Paragraph 1]: "...Consequently, multiple avenues exist for targeted therapeutic intervention."  
  [Start of Paragraph 2]: "Cognitive behavioral therapy has been widely utilized in clinical practice..."
- ✅ [End of Paragraph 1]: "...Consequently, multiple avenues exist for targeted therapeutic intervention."  
  [Start of Paragraph 2]: "Among these potential interventions, cognitive behavioral therapy represents the most widely validated approach..."

## 7. Hedging Problems

### Excessive Hedging (Throat-Clearing)

Excessive qualifiers erode authorial authority and generate verbose ambiguity:

- ❌ "It would seem to suggest that perhaps there might possibly be some indication that social media could potentially affect mental health outcomes in some populations."
- ✅ "These findings suggest that social media use affects mental health outcomes in vulnerable populations."

### Insufficient Hedging (Overgeneralization)

Absolutist assertions overstep empirical warrant and invite immediate peer rejection:

- ❌ "This study proves that regular exercise prevents clinical depression."
- ✅ "This study indicates that regular aerobic exercise reduces depressive symptoms; however, longitudinal trials are required to establish causal thresholds."

## 8. Terminology Issues

### Undefined Specialized Terms and Acronyms

Always expand acronyms and provide conceptual definitions upon first usage:

- ❌ "We used SEM to examine the structural parameters."
- ✅ "We utilized Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to examine the structural parameters."

### Inconsistent Conceptual Terminology (Elegant Variation)

Avoid shifting terms for aesthetic variety; technical constructs require rigid terminological consistency:

- ❌ Alternating arbitrarily between "participants", "subjects", "respondents", and "interviewees" to denote the exact same group.
- ✅ Select a single standardized term ("participants") and apply it consistently across all chapters (Terminology SOT).

## 9. Paragraph Structure Problems

### Missing Topic Sentences

Begin each paragraph with an overarching analytical claim rather than isolated citations:

- ❌ "Smith (2020) observed a positive correlation. Jones (2021) reported similar effects. Brown (2022) extended these findings."
- ✅ "Recent empirical studies consistently demonstrate a robust positive relationship between variable X and variable Y. Smith (2020) first documented this correlation in laboratory settings. Jones (2021) replicated the effect in field experiments, and Brown (2022) extended the framework to international samples."

### Multi-Topic Disorganized Paragraphs

Avoid packing multiple divergent themes into an undifferentiated block:

- ❌ Combining theoretical background, participant recruitment criteria, statistical protocols, and research ethics into a single 400-word paragraph.
- ✅ Separate the discussion into discrete paragraphs, each governed by its own topic sentence.

## 10. Advanced Stylistic and Structural Traps

### Prepositional Chaining and Stacking

Strings of prepositional phrases slow cognitive processing and create rhythmic monotony:

- ❌ "A study of the evaluation of the effects of the implementation of educational policy on student performance..."
- ✅ "Evaluating how educational policy implementation affects student performance..."

### Expletive Phrasing and Dummy Pronouns

Avoid postponing the real grammatical subject behind empty dummy constructions:

- ❌ "It was determined by the committee that there were sufficient grounds for..."
- ✅ "The committee determined that sufficient grounds existed for..."

### Hidden Action (Masked Verbs)

Ensure the central action of the clause is expressed directly as a verb rather than buried inside nominal compounds:

- ❌ "The advisory board arrived at an agreement concerning the allocation of funding."
- ✅ "The advisory board agreed on allocating funding."

### Superfluous Modifiers and Tautologies

Purge empty modifiers that dilute scholarly precision:

- ❌ "The two groups displayed an extremely unique divergence that was very noticeable."
- ✅ "The two groups diverged markedly."

### Vague Demonstrative Openers

Anchor demonstratives with explicit conceptual nouns to maintain logical cohesion:

- ❌ "This proves that the hypothesis was correct."
- ✅ "This empirical convergence corroborates the primary hypothesis."

## Quick Reference: Revision Strategies

### Paramedic Method (Conciseness)
1. Identify and circle prepositional phrases.
2. Locate linking verbs ("is", "are", "was", "were").
3. Uncover the real conceptual action and convert it into a strong active verb.
4. Reposition the grammatical subject directly adjacent to the main verb.
5. Delete redundant modifiers and dead-weight stock expressions.
6. Eliminate pleonasms and tautological pairings.
7. Switch to active voice unless passive voice is formally justified.

### Clarity Optimization
1. Ensure the grammatical subject of every sentence is immediately identifiable.
2. Position the subject near the sentence opening.
3. Maintain minimal distance between subject and predicate.
4. Use active voice when recounting researcher decisions and empirical insights.
5. Decompose sentences exceeding 25 words into discrete logical units.
6. Restrict each sentence to a single central proposition.

### Academic Rigor
1. Provide formal definitions for specialized constructs upon first appearance.
2. Expand all acronyms at first mention.
3. Substantiate every theoretical claim and empirical generalization with citations.
4. Maintain a consistent, dignified scholarly register.
5. Adhere strictly to disciplinary reporting conventions and citation standards.

### Progressive Multi-Pass Review Strategy
- **First Pass (Macro Level)**: Structural cohesion, topic sentence hierarchy, logical progression of argument.
- **Second Pass (Sentence Level)**: Subject-verb alignment and proximity, sentence length distribution, active/passive balance.
- **Third Pass (Word Level)**: Wordiness reduction, nominalization conversion, verb strength, terminological consistency.
- **Final Pass (Proofing)**: Transitional precision, style guide compliance, typographical and citation integrity.
