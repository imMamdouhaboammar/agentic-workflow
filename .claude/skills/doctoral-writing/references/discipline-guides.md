# Discipline-Specific Writing Conventions

A guide to doctoral-level writing conventions across academic disciplines. While core principles of clarity and conciseness apply universally, conventions for authorial voice, structural organization, citation styles, and terminological usage vary substantially across scholarly fields.

> **Role**: Disciplinary conventions guide (WHERE — Disciplinary Context). For governing principles, see `SKILL.md`; for resolving active vs. passive voice conflicts, see "Resolving Principle Conflicts (P9 Intersections)" in `SKILL.md`.

---

## Overview: Cross-Disciplinary Comparison

| Dimension | Humanities | Social Sciences | Natural Sciences & Engineering |
|-----------|------------|-----------------|--------------------------------|
| **Authorial Voice** | First person frequently permitted and valued | Third person predominant; first person selectively permitted | Passive voice traditional; active voice increasingly favored |
| **Citation Style** | MLA, Chicago (Notes & Bibliography) | APA, Chicago (Author-Date) | CSE, IEEE, APA, journal-specific |
| **Literature Review** | Integrated throughout narrative chapters | Standalone literature review chapter | Compact synthesis within Introduction |
| **Theoretical Framing** | Central, hermeneutic, interpretive | Explicit theoretical framework section | Focused on empirical models or mechanisms |
| **Methodological Detail** | Varies; textual, archival, philosophical | Extensive, standardized, replicable | Highly granular, standardized protocols |
| **Results Presentation** | Narrative, discursive, interpretive | Tabular, statistical, narrative | Figures, tables, quantitative data-centric |
| **Argumentation Style** | Interpretive, close-reading, contextual | Hypothesis-driven, deductive/inductive | Empirical, experimental, objective |

## 1. Humanities

### General Characteristics

**Writing Style:**
- First-person narration is widely accepted and often preferred when establishing interpretive stance.
- Heavy emphasis on hermeneutic depth, argumentation, and textual evidence.
- Sustained theoretical engagement with foundational and contemporary theorists.
- Longer, structurally intricate sentences are tolerated when managing nuanced philosophical distinctions, provided clause relationships remain unambiguous.
- Scholarly elegance and rhetorical craftsmanship are valued alongside analytical rigor.

**Structural Organization:**
- Less standardized than STEM or social science manuscripts.
- Literature reviews are frequently woven organically into thematic chapters rather than isolated.
- Chapters are organized around themes, historical periods, or textual corpuses rather than fixed methodological steps.
- Extensive quotation and detailed close reading of primary sources.

### Authorial Voice and Person

**First-Person Usage (Appropriate):**
- "I argue that this text complicates traditional binary models of..."
- "In this chapter, I examine how archival records reveal..."
- "My reading demonstrates that the protagonist's silence functions as..."

**Third-Person Attribution (Prior Scholarship):**
- "Morrison contends that historical trauma operates as..."
- "The historical record demonstrates an abrupt institutional shift in..."

### Citation Conventions

**Primary Styles:**
- **MLA (9th ed.)**: Literature, cultural studies, modern languages, and cinema studies.
- **Chicago (17th ed., Notes & Bibliography)**: History, art history, philosophy, and religious studies.
- **Turabian**: General academic monographs in humanities disciplines.

**MLA In-Text Citation:**
- "As Morrison suggests, 'memory is a form of restoration' (234)."

**Chicago Footnotes:**
- First note: Toni Morrison, *Beloved* (New York: Knopf, 1987), 234.
- Subsequent notes: Morrison, *Beloved*, 245.

### Terminology Management

**Defining Theoretical Constructs Upon Introduction:**
- ❌ "The text employs heteroglossia throughout."
- ✅ "The text employs heteroglossia—Bakhtin's concept of multiple, competing social voices within a single narrative—to subvert monolithic authority."

### Major Subfields

- **Literary Studies**: Close reading, theoretical framework integration, historical contextualization, prosodic and narrative analysis.
- **History**: Archival provenance, historiographical engagement, chronological clarity, dense footnote citation.
- **Philosophy**: Rigorous conceptual definition, premise-conclusion argument structures, anticipation of counterexamples, precise logical syntax.

## 2. Social Sciences

### General Characteristics

**Writing Style:**
- Third person is predominant, though first-person plural ("we") is standard in multi-author studies and first-person singular ("I") is increasingly welcomed in qualitative research.
- Empirical grounding and systematic methodology are paramount.
- Clear articulation of research questions, hypotheses, and theoretical frameworks.
- Highly standardized macro-structure balancing accessibility with technical accuracy.

**Structural Organization:**
- Rigid five-chapter or standard IMRaD model: Introduction → Literature Review / Theoretical Framework → Methodology → Results → Discussion & Conclusion.
- Dedicated standalone literature review evaluating competing empirical findings.
- Transparent operationalization of variables and constructs.

### Authorial Voice and Person

**Third-Person Conventions (Most Common):**
- "This study examines the relationship between work autonomy and employee retention."
- "Participants reported elevated levels of cognitive fatigue following the task."

**First-Person Conventions (Selectively Permitted):**
- "We hypothesized that perceived autonomy would moderate this relationship."
- "I conducted semi-structured interviews with 24 municipal administrators."
- *Note*: Always verify department, committee, or journal preferences regarding authorial person.

### Citation Conventions (APA 7th Edition)

**In-Text Formats:**
- Single author: (Smith, 2022)
- Two authors: (Smith & Jones, 2022)
- Three or more authors: (Smith et al., 2022)
- Parenthetical group citation: (Jones, 2021; Smith, 2022)

**Scholarly Reporting Verbs:**
- *Empirical outcomes*: found, demonstrated, revealed, established
- *Theoretical propositions*: argued, proposed, theorized, posited
- *Direct claims*: contended, asserted, maintained

### Methodology Section Mandatory Elements

1. **Research Design**: Explicit statement of design (experimental, quasi-experimental, longitudinal survey, phenomenological, mixed-methods).
2. **Participants & Sampling Frame**: Target population, sampling technique, demographic profile, attrition rates, statistical power justification.
3. **Measures & Instrumentation**: Operational definitions, source scales, sample items, response scales, validity and reliability coefficients (Cronbach's α, McDonald's ω).
4. **Procedure**: Chronological, step-by-step documentation allowing independent replication.
5. **Analytical Strategy**: Concrete statistical tests, software versions, estimation methods (e.g., maximum likelihood), and handling of missing data.

### Results vs. Discussion

**Results Section:**
- Present data objectively without interpretive extrapolation.
- Exclude normative commentary and theoretical speculation.
- Rely on high-density statistical tables and figures.
- Report full statistical parameters: *t*(149) = 5.67, *p* < .001, *d* = 1.15.

**Discussion Section:**
- Interpret empirical findings in direct conversation with the literature.
- Disclose methodological boundaries and limitations candidly.
- Delineate actionable theoretical, policy, and practical implications.
- Propose specific, fertile avenues for future research.

### Statistical Reporting Standards

**Required Metrics:**
- Exact test statistic and degrees of freedom.
- Exact *p*-value (e.g., *p* = .023, not *p* < .05, unless *p* < .001).
- Standardized effect size (Cohen's *d*, partial η², odds ratio, Pearson's *r*).
- Descriptive statistics (means and standard deviations: *M*, *SD*).

**Standard Model:**
"An independent-samples *t*-test revealed a statistically significant difference between cohorts (*M*₁ = 78.3, *SD*₁ = 12.1; *M*₀ = 65.2, *SD*₀ = 14.3), *t*(148) = 5.67, *p* < .001, Cohen's *d* = 1.02."

### Major Subfields

- **Psychology**: APA compliance mandatory, operational construct definitions, robust statistical reporting, preregistration disclosure.
- **Sociology**: Quantitative, qualitative, or mixed designs; explicit sociological frameworks; APA or Chicago author-date.
- **Education**: Action research, policy evaluation, pedagogical interventions, mixed methodologies, practitioner implications.
- **Economics**: Econometric modeling, identification strategies, instrumental variables, formal mathematical proofs, dense regression tables.
- **Political Science**: Quantitative modeling, comparative case studies, formal game theory, institutional process tracing.

## 3. Natural Sciences & Engineering

### General Characteristics

**Writing Style:**
- Rigorously objective, data-centric, and concise.
- Passive voice has long been traditional in procedural sections, but active voice is increasingly preferred for clarity and economy.
- Uncompromising focus on reproducibility and precision.
- Highly standardized scientific nomenclature and mathematical notation.

**Structural Organization:**
- Classic IMRaD structure: Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion.
- Brief, targeted literature overview embedded in the Introduction.
- Granular, exhaustive Materials and Methods section.
- Figure- and table-centric Results reporting.
- Concise Discussion contextualizing findings within broader physical/biological frameworks.

### Authorial Voice and Person

**Passive Voice (Traditional, Procedural Sections):**
- "Samples were synthesized according to standard sol-gel protocols."
- "Reaction temperatures were maintained at 350 °C for 120 minutes."

**Active Voice (Modern Preference, Explanatory Sections):**
- "We synthesized novel nanocrystals to investigate surface catalysis."
- "These data demonstrate that ligand exchange alters electronic coupling."
- *Note*: Major publishers (Nature, Science, IEEE, Elsevier) now encourage active voice to enhance directness and eliminate syntactic ambiguity.

### Citation Conventions

**Primary Styles:**
- **CSE (Council of Science Editors)**: Biology, biochemistry, environmental sciences (Citation-Sequence, Citation-Name, or Name-Year).
- **IEEE**: Electrical engineering, computer science, telecommunications, robotics (bracketed sequential numbering: [1], [2]).
- **ACS / AIP / APS**: Chemistry and physics specialized standards.

### Materials and Methods Precision

**Exhaustive Specification Required:**
- Chemical reagents, purity grades, and suppliers with geographic locations.
- Complete biological strains, cell lines, and culture media parameters.
- Analytical instrument models, software versions, and operating conditions.
- Step-by-step procedural parameters (temperatures, pressures, flow rates, duration).

**Comparison Example:**
- ❌ "Samples were heated and then analyzed."
- ✅ "Samples were annealed at 350 °C for 2 h in a muffle furnace (Model KSL-1100X, MTI Corp., Richmond, CA) under ambient atmosphere. Following cooling to room temperature, phase purity was characterized by X-ray diffraction (Rigaku MiniFlex 600, Cu Kα radiation, λ = 1.5406 Å) at a scan rate of 2° min⁻¹ from 10° to 80° 2θ."

### Results Section Standards

- Center findings around publication-quality figures, schematics, and tables.
- Keep narrative prose focused strictly on guiding the reader through the data.
- State uncertainty intervals and error metrics explicitly: "The average product yield was 85.3 ± 2.1% (mean ± SD, *n* = 5)."

### Nomenclature and Units

- **Chemistry**: Follow IUPAC systematic nomenclature.
- **Molecular Biology**: Adhere to official gene and protein formatting (*BRCA1* in italics for gene; BRCA1 in roman for protein).
- **Taxonomy**: Use binomial nomenclature in italics (*Homo sapiens*, *Escherichia coli*).
- **Units**: Adhere to the International System of Units (SI); maintain correct spacing between numbers and units (25 °C, not 25°C; 10 mM, not 10mM).

## 4. Mixed and Interdisciplinary Fields

### Strategies for Interdisciplinary Writing

**Strategy 1: Follow Target Journal or Department Standards**
- Submitting to an empirical psychology journal → Follow APA standards.
- Submitting to a cultural history journal → Follow Chicago Notes & Bibliography.
- Interdisciplinary journals (e.g., *Nature Human Behaviour*, *PLOS ONE*) → Review specific author guidelines.

**Strategy 2: Integrate Methodological Norms**
- For qualitative strands: Adopt descriptive, reflexive, and narrative presentation.
- For quantitative strands: Maintain structured, tabular, and inferential reporting.
- For mixed designs: Articulate explicit convergence protocols uniting both strands.

**Key Best Practices:**
1. State your methodological and theoretical positioning clearly in the Introduction.
2. Define boundary concepts whose definitions differ across collaborating disciplines.
3. Organize the primary narrative for your target readership, consigning specialized disciplinary derivations to technical appendices.

## 5. Comparative Register and Global Scholarly Conventions

### Maintaining Formal Scholarly Register

Doctoral prose requires sustained adherence to an international scholarly register:

- **Third-Person Structural Framing**:  
  Prefer "This dissertation investigates...", "The present study evaluates...", or "The empirical evidence demonstrates..."
- **Elevated Analytical Vocabulary**:  
  Replace colloquial formulations with established scholarly terminology across all chapters:
  - *Introduction*: Establish "empirical lacunae", "scholarly consensus", "compelling research problems".
  - *Literature Review*: Group prior work into "theoretical streams", "competing paradigms", "boundary conditions".
  - *Methodology*: Detail "sampling frames", "operational definitions", "measurement fidelity", "triangulation".
  - *Results*: Present "statistically significant disparities", "covariance patterns", "robust regression weights".
  - *Discussion*: Delineate "theoretical ramifications", "generalizability constraints", "normative implications".

### Expressions to Purge from Scholarly Prose

| Informal / Colloquial Phrasing | Calibrated Scholarly Alternative |
|--------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| seems like it might be | appears to indicate, suggests |
| we tried to see | we investigated, we evaluated |
| a lot of studies | extensive empirical literature, numerous investigations |
| good / bad results | favorable / adverse outcomes, nominal performance |
| at the end of the day | ultimately, in conclusion, on balance |
| comes down to | hinges upon, depends centrally on |

## 6. Citation Style Quick Reference

### APA 7th Edition
- **Book**: Smith, J. (2022). *Title of book* (2nd ed.). Academic Press.
- **Journal Article**: Jones, A., & Lee, B. (2022). Title of article. *Journal of Applied Psychology*, *45*(3), 123–145. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxx
- **Edited Volume Chapter**: Brown, C. (2022). Chapter title. In D. Editor (Ed.), *Current perspectives in sociology* (pp. 50–75). Routledge.

### MLA 9th Edition
- **Book**: Smith, John. *Title of Book*. 2nd ed., Academic Press, 2022.
- **Journal Article**: Jones, Alice, and Bob Lee. "Title of Article." *Journal of Modern Literature*, vol. 45, no. 3, 2022, pp. 123–45.

### Chicago 17th Edition (Notes & Bibliography)
- **First Footnote**: John Smith, *Title of Book*, 2nd ed. (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2022), 45.
- **Subsequent Footnote**: Smith, *Title of Book*, 67.
- **Bibliography**: Smith, John. *Title of Book*. 2nd ed. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2022.

### CSE (Citation-Sequence)
- **In-Text**: "...as reported previously (1, 3, 5)."
- **Reference List**: 1. Smith J. Title of book. 2nd ed. Philadelphia (PA): Academic Press; 2022. 250 p.

### IEEE
- **In-Text**: "...as demonstrated in recent robotics implementations [1], [4]."
- **Reference List**: [1] J. Smith and B. Jones, "Autonomous navigation algorithms," *IEEE Trans. Robot.*, vol. 38, no. 2, pp. 112–125, Apr. 2022.

## 7. Adapting to Specific Target Journals

### Systematic 4-Step Adaptation Workflow

1. **Obtain Author Guidelines**: Download the official Guide for Authors from the journal's publication portal.
2. **Examine Recent Articles**: Analyze 3–5 representative articles published within the last 12 months to discern implicit stylistic norms.
3. **Audit Structural Constraints**: Note word limits, abstract formatting (structured vs. unstructured), keyword caps, and graphic conventions.
4. **Configure Reference Manager**: Set your citation manager (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) to the journal's exact CSL style.

### Red Flags That Trigger Desk Rejection

- ❌ Non-compliance with journal citation style and bibliographic format.
- ❌ Exceeding word, figure, or table limitations.
- ❌ Submitting a manuscript outside the stated aims and scope of the journal.
- ❌ Deficient engagement with foundational literature published in the target journal.
- ❌ Insufficient methodological detail preventing experimental or statistical replication.
- ❌ Vague, unsupported, or over-hedged empirical conclusions.

## 8. Working with Multilingual and Translated Manuscripts

### Adapting Translated Work into International Doctoral English

Scholars adapting manuscripts across languages should apply four core transformations:

1. **Sentence Length Decomposition**: Regional academic traditions often favor multi-clause composite sentences. Decompose extended constructions into concise 15–25 word sentences.
2. **Passive-to-Active Conversion**: Convert sluggish, indirect passive chaining into active grammatical agency where appropriate.
3. **Explicit Subject Insertion**: Ensure every clause possesses an identifiable grammatical subject, avoiding unanchored verbal predicates.
4. **Epistemic Hedging Calibration**: Convert habitual or apologetic vernacular hedges into standardized, precise scholarly epistemic markers.

**Diagnostic Transformations:**
- ❌ "In this study, a survey was conducted and data collection was accomplished by the researchers."
- ✅ "We surveyed 320 professionals and analyzed the resulting dataset."

- ❌ "It is thought that there could possibly be an indication that variable X influences variable Y."
- ✅ "These empirical findings indicate that variable X influences variable Y."

## Summary: Universal Principles Across All Disciplines

Regardless of disciplinary divergence, every doctoral manuscript must fulfill these seven universal criteria:

1. ✓ **Clarity**: Readers grasp conceptual meaning without re-reading or guessing authorial intent.
2. ✓ **Precision**: Technical terms are defined explicitly; empirical quantities and metrics are exact.
3. ✓ **Evidence**: Every theoretical assertion and empirical claim is grounded in data or authoritative citations.
4. ✓ **Logical Flow**: Arguments unfold sequentially with clear transitions between sentences, paragraphs, and chapters.
5. ✓ **Consistency**: Style, terminology, citation syntax, and authorial voice remain unified throughout (Absolute Criterion 2).
6. ✓ **Calibrated Detail**: Methodological and theoretical detail is sufficient for expert evaluation without superfluous clutter.
7. ✓ **Ethical Integrity**: Proper intellectual attribution is provided, and empirical limitations are disclosed transparently.
